Carrier anxiety, medication battles, and subcutaneous fluid struggles — most cat owners know these all too well. I used to be jealous of people with "angel cats." But what I've come to realize is that with consistent training, most cats can become someone else's idea of an angel.

When I first got my white cat, I had no experience and barely took her anywhere. As a young adult, she was terrifying outside the home — she scratched and bit both me and the vet more than once. It made every vet visit stressful: I was worried she'd attack me, and even more worried she'd attack the vet.

Now, with a senior cat with a chronic illness who needs regular vet visits, I've deeply felt the value of training these three things early — during the kitten stage or when you first bring a new cat home. It saves enormous amounts of tears and stress down the road.

1. **Carrier Training**

   * Keep the carrier in the living room as a "cat den." Put a soft mat, toys, and catnip inside so they treat it as a safe base.
   * Leave the door open always — let them come and go freely. Give treats or feed meals inside.
   * Never bring out the carrier only when it's time for the vet. That teaches them to associate it with scary things.

2. **Medication Training**

   * Use positive reinforcement from day one: hide pills in treats, meat paste, or prescription food; use a pill gun with a big reward afterward.
   * Give medication in the same spot, with the same calm tone, every time — so they learn "medication = good things happen."
   * My white cat initially refused everything. I started giving her wet food immediately after medication. Now she comes to remind *me* when it's time.

3. **Daily Body Handling**

   * Spend 1–2 minutes each day touching their ears, checking their teeth, pressing their paw pads, stroking their belly — always paired with treats. This makes vet examinations much less frightening.
   * After a full year of consistent vet visits and home training based on their advice, both my cats now get through appointments without attacking anyone. My white cat — once fiercer than the orange cat — now actually explores the vet clinic more willingly than he does.

**Advice for new cat owners:**

* Start now — it's never too early!
* Even if your cat is already an adult or a senior, don't give up. Rebuilding associations one small step at a time, with positive reinforcement, still works.
