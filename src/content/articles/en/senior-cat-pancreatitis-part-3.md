**This is a personal experience share. For any health concerns or questions about lab results, always consult your veterinarian!**

The thing most feared with pancreatitis is gastrointestinal bleeding and severe diarrhea. While she was hospitalized, her hematocrit (red blood cell percentage) dropped into the low 20s%. The vet administered an erythropoietin injection and monitored her — thankfully, it eventually rose. After discharge, she continued to have diarrhea, and her hematocrit slowly dropped again. Another injection on the follow-up visit stabilized things.

The inflammation marker SAA stubbornly remained high. The vet asked if I'd consider a **plasma transfusion** — hoping it would help her fight the disease, but noting there was a risk of allergic reaction. Every option had risk. Each decision was agonizing.

The deadline for deciding was the following day. I said I wanted to watch one more day — if the numbers didn't drop, we'd do the plasma transfusion. I had no real hope. It had been so many days without improvement; I was mentally prepared that the transfusion was inevitable. Then the next day — the values miraculously improved. Still elevated, but trending in the right direction. We held off on the plasma transfusion. Lucky. And she was incredibly strong.

Another concern: pancreatitis combined with chronic kidney disease can cause dangerously low blood pressure, leading to insufficient organ perfusion — which dramatically raises the risk of death. This was the primary reason I insisted on the **24-hour hospital** — with medical staff monitoring overnight, any emergency could be addressed immediately.

#### What This Experience Taught Me
- At 18 years old, any sign of something "off" requires immediate veterinary attention
- There is no specific cure for pancreatitis; only supportive care (pain relief, hydration, nutrition, anti-diarrheal)
- NG tubes are difficult to manage, but for a cat that cannot eat on their own, they are life-saving
- Choosing a 24-hour care hospital truly matters

She's home resting now, and her values are slowly stabilizing — but continued monitoring of kidney function and gastrointestinal health is still needed. Every day she's with me becomes more precious. She's worth every ounce of effort. ❤️
