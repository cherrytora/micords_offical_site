#### **This is a personal experience share. For any questions about your cat's health or lab results, always consult your veterinarian!**

After a feline health checkup, the most common test is a **blood panel**. Receiving that report full of English abbreviations and numbers is, understandably, confusing for most pet owners.

## Why Regular Blood Tests Matter

Cats are excellent at hiding pain. Many diseases show no obvious signs in the early stages. Regular bloodwork helps to:

* Detect kidney and liver problems early
* Monitor the progression of chronic conditions (like CKD or diabetes)
* Confirm how the body is responding to medication

## Common Blood Test Values

### BUN / Creatinine (Kidney Markers)

BUN (blood urea nitrogen) and Creatinine are the two most important indicators of kidney function. Elevated values may indicate reduced kidney filtration capacity.

In the early stages of kidney disease, both values may still fall within the normal range — so **trends matter more than any single result**.

### ALT / AST (Liver Markers)

Elevated ALT is typically associated with liver cell damage; elevated AST may be related to liver or muscle problems.

### RBC / HCT (Red Blood Cells / Hematocrit)

These assess whether anemia is present. Secondary anemia is common in cats with chronic kidney disease.

## Tracking Blood Test Values with Micords

Every time you receive a blood test report, you can enter the values directly into Micords' "Blood Test Record" feature. At the next vet visit:

1. Open Micords → Home
2. Select the value you want to review (e.g., BUN) — the trend chart displays immediately
3. Tap the detail view next to the chart to see all markers' trends at once

This way, instead of just saying "this one is higher than last time," you can show your vet a 6-month trend chart — making conversations more efficient and informed.
