**This is a personal experience share. For any health concerns or questions about lab results, always consult your veterinarian!**

After the diagnosis, I was faced with two main treatment paths:

#### Option 1: Lifelong Medication
- Give medication at a fixed time every day, without missing doses
- Medication only controls thyroid hormone production; the gland itself continues to enlarge
- Dosage may need to increase over time; requires long-term TT4 and kidney monitoring

#### Option 2: Radioactive Iodine Therapy (I-131)
- In Taiwan, only one hospital in Taichung offers this treatment
- The only treatment with a realistic chance of a **cure**
- My cat was 13 years old (relatively young for a hyperthyroid cat), and his levels weren't critically high — the vet said he was a good candidate

I chose **radioactive iodine therapy**.

#### The Pre-Treatment Preparation Process
Once I decided, I compiled all his bloodwork from diagnosis onwards and emailed it to the Taichung hospital. They reviewed everything, called to explain the process, and scheduled the pre-treatment workup.

**Required tests before treatment:**
- Full bloodwork (TT4, kidney function, liver function, etc.)
- Chest X-ray + abdominal X-ray
- Cardiac ultrasound (recommended to see a cardiac specialist)

**Important note:** Medication must be stopped 5–7 days before the procedure. If the hyperthyroidism is poorly controlled, stopping medication during this period can trigger a "thyroid storm," which carries significant risk.\
Fortunately, his test results came back showing only mild kidney shrinkage — everything else was relatively stable.

The hospital gave a thorough explanation of the costs, hospitalization process, and what to expect. Because of the isotope's half-life restrictions, once the appointment is booked it can't be easily rescheduled, and a deposit is required in advance. I booked time off work before confirming the date — because there really is no backing out.

The waiting period between "deciding to do it" and "actually doing it" was the hardest part. I was worried and scared, up at night googling everything.

### Micords Tracking Reminder
During the waiting period before radioactive iodine therapy, keeping up with [Micords records](https://cherrytora.github.io/micords_offical_site/tutorial/) is especially important!\
It gives you a clear picture of changes before and after stopping medication, and helps prevent symptoms from spiraling out of control.

**Recommended items to track:**
- Daily weight
- Water intake and urine output
- Appetite and food intake
- Vocalization and activity level
- Medication status (especially important after stopping medication)

This not only gives your vet more complete data — it also gives you peace of mind.
